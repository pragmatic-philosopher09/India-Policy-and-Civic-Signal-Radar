How the momentum score works

No black box. Here is the whole method; you can recompute any number on the site from the evidence lists.

1. Source

Every month PRS Legislative Research, a non-partisan Indian think-tank, publishes a Monthly Policy Review listing every significant government action — bills, rules, committee reports, drafts, court rulings — grouped by ministry. It is licensed CC BY 4.0, so we can reuse it with attribution. We fetch each review once (respecting their crawl delay) and keep the history.

2. Tagging

Each action is matched against a small keyword list per topic (title matches count 3×, body matches 1×, a matching PRS ministry heading adds 1). Anything scoring ≥ 4 becomes evidence for that topic. Keyword lists are public in the repo and can be argued with.

3. Weighting by what kind of action it is

A law being passed matters more than a committee recommending something. Each action gets a weight:

ActionWeight
Law passed×3.0
Bill introduced×2.0
Rules notified×2.0
Cabinet approval×1.8
Open for comment×1.5
Court ruling×1.5
Committee report×1.2
Scheme / programme×1.2
Update×1.0

4. Momentum

For each topic we sum weights per month, then compare the average of the last 3 months against the 6 months before. The % change is what you see on each card.

5. The 0–100 score

A convenience number for sorting, built from three parts: 45% momentum (log of recent/baseline ratio, squashed), 40% recent volume (saturates at ~12 weighted points/month), 15% breadth (how many different ministries acted recently, saturates at 4). Status labels: Heating up ≥ +50%, Cooling ≤ −33%, Quiet when recent activity is under 1 point/month.

Known limits

  • One source. PRS is excellent but a second independent source (PIB press releases) is planned for cross-validation.
  • Keyword tagging misses synonyms and can over-match generic words. Evidence is shown so you can spot this.
  • Plain-English summaries are generated by an AI model or extracted from the first sentence; the linked primary document is always authoritative.
