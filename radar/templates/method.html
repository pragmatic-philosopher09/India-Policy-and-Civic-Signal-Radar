How the momentum score works

No black box. Here is the whole method; you can recompute any number on the site from the evidence lists.

1. Source

Every month PRS Legislative Research, a non-partisan Indian think-tank, publishes a Monthly Policy Review listing every significant government action — bills, rules, committee reports, drafts, court rulings — grouped by ministry. It is licensed CC BY 4.0, so we can reuse it with attribution. We fetch each review once (respecting their crawl delay) and keep the history.

2. Tagging

Each action is matched against a small keyword list per topic (title matches count 3×, body matches 1×, a matching PRS ministry heading adds 1). Anything scoring ≥ 4 becomes evidence for that topic. Keyword lists are public in the repo and can be argued with.

3. Weighting by what kind of action it is

4. Momentum

For each topic we sum weights per month, then compare the average of the last {{ recent_window }} months against the {{ baseline_window }} months before. The % change is what you see on each card. Labels: Heating up ≥ +50%, Cooling ≤ −33%, Quiet when recent activity is under 1 weighted point/month, otherwise Steady.

5. Confidence

A percentage on a handful of documents looks more precise than it is, so every signal carries a confidence label:

  • Low — fewer than 8 tagged actions in total, or fewer than 2 in the recent window. One document can flip the label.
  • Medium — fewer than 20 actions, or fewer than 3 ministries involved, or over 80% of recent activity in a single month (a parliamentary-session effect).
  • High — 20+ actions, 3+ ministries, activity spread across months.

Until a second independent source is added, nothing is rated above what the evidence count allows, and every topic page states the "single source" limitation.

6. Ordering

Cards are ordered by an internal composite (45% momentum, 40% recent volume, 15% breadth), exposed as sort_score in the JSON. We deliberately don't display it as a headline number: it would imply that a topic with more activity is more important, which the data cannot support.

7. "Open for comment" deadlines

We extract the comment deadline from PRS's summary text when it is stated ("comments invited by 4 September 2026"). Items with a passed deadline are moved to "recently closed"; items where PRS didn't state a date are shown with a "check source" flag. Always verify on the ministry's page — deadlines get extended.

Known limits

  • This measures formal government action, not public attention. PRS records bills, rules, committee reports and drafts. It does not record implementation, strikes, court petitions or news coverage. A topic can be "Quiet" here while dominating headlines.
  • One source. A second independent source (PIB press releases) is planned so signals can be cross-checked.
  • Keyword tagging misses synonyms and can over-match generic words. Evidence is shown so you can spot this.
  • Plain-English summaries are generated by an AI model or extracted from the first sentence; the linked primary document is always authoritative.
